How a number gets onto a page

1
Fetch politely, once a second
robots.txt first, including any crawl delay a site asks for, and only pages linked from pages already held.
2
Archive the raw bytes before reading them
Every document is stored under the hash of its contents, so a parse can always be re-run against exactly what was published.
3
Parse, then try to disprove the parse
A file that agrees with its own totals can still be wrong. Independent checks compare it with a second source wherever one exists.
4
Keep what fails, and say so
Untrusted rows are not deleted. They are marked, counted and kept out of the analysis, with the reason attached.
5
Show the lineage
Every page names the files it read and the checks those files passed, so any number here can be traced back to a document.

What is missing, and why

9 matches have no box score
Iowa, Kentucky, Northwestern, Purdue, UCLA. Their schools publish those pages only as JavaScript apps that ship no numbers, so nothing was fetched.
4 more are linked but not parsed
The 2016 StatCrew HTML box scores: the page holds the numbers, the parser finds four blocks where it expects two.
237 player lines are left out as untrusted
They are the failing team block of 22 files whose printed totals do not reconcile; the 208 rows of the block that does reconcile are kept.
223 matches have a box score but no rally log
657 of 880 have rally play-by-play. Before 2016 no rally log is published at all; season totals reach back to 2000 and schedules to 1975.
37 differences from the season reports are unexplained
Of 412 differences in all, 382 are matches this corpus does not have. The rest are listed, not settled by preference.
The strongest check we have runs backwards

The official season report prints every player's season line. Summing the per-match rows has to reproduce it, player by player and column by column. 76 of 139 lines match exactly, and all 412 differences are written down with a reason: 382 of them turned out to be matches that were missing, which is how we learned what to go and find.

What the data refuses to answer

17 questions the exports say outright they cannot answer. A refusal is a finding: it is why a metric is absent here rather than estimated. Each one is quoted from the export that refused it.

Question Refused by Why
metrics.first_ball_side_out rally_analytics.json a first-ball side-out can only be separated from a transition side-out by the touches inside the rally, and no source records them: all 86174 rally lines in this study print one terminal action each ({'kill by': 51359, 'attack error': 21504, 'service error': 7664, 'service ace': 3971, 'ball handling error': 884, 'bad set': 790, 'other': 2}), never a dig, a set or a second attack. The huskers.com PDFs carry first-ball numbers in an 'Enhanced Insights' section, and that section survives in 0 of the 880 extracted corpus files, so it is a separate parser and a separate collection job, not something this play-by-play can be asked for.
Instead: the terminal action does say how each reception ended, which separates a side-out the team earned from one the serving team handed over, and names the only class that certainly needed no touch at all (the opponent's service error)
was this a tough serve? how many serves put the opponent out of system? serving.json no source in this corpus rates a pass. Every rally line prints one action, the one that ended the rally; a serve that forced an overpass and a serve that was passed to the setter's hands are the same row here unless one of them was an ace. There is no 0-3 pass rating, no 'in system' flag and no reception line anywhere in the three sources.
float or jump? which zone was it served to? how fast? serving.json none of it is recorded. The huskers.com PDFs, the 2016 StatCrew pages and the opponents' Sidearm pages all print the server, the outcome and the score, and nothing about the serve itself.
who received it? serving.json only an ace names the player it beat, and it names her inside the text ('Service Ace (17 Swain, Jayhlin)'). A serve that lands and is passed names nobody, so serving at a weak passer cannot be measured, and neither can the share of a team's serves aimed at one receiver.
how much of the rally after a landed serve does the server deserve? serving.json the rally's touches are not recorded, so the server's share of it cannot be separated from the passer's, the setter's and the hitter's. This module measures whether the conversion depends on who served (it does not, measurably) and refuses to split the credit that is left.
a career table for the 2016 Rolfzen serves serving.json the four 2016 StatCrew pages print no jersey in a rally text and both twins served, so 52 serves resolve to a surname two players share. They stay a pooled 2016 row and get no career row: merging them would invent a player and splitting them would invent a number.
serve numbers for the seasons and matches with no play-by-play serving.json this study can only use the 148 matches that have a rally record at all. The official season reports in season_totals.json carry each player's season SA and SE back to 2000 and would extend the ace and error halves of this table - but they carry no rally, so the conversion, the point-scoring rate and therefore the expected value cannot be built from them. Joining the two is a separate job and is not done here.
strength of schedule against the rest of the country opponent_adjusted.json every match in data/reports/box_scores.json is a Nebraska match, so no opponent's games against a third team are anywhere in this corpus. What is computed here is relative to the opponents Nebraska itself played, and nothing in it can say whether those opponents are strong nationally.
an opponent effect for a team we hold once opponent_adjusted.json 57 of the 138 opponents appear in exactly one match. Leaving that match out leaves nothing, so those matches carry no baseline and are excluded from every adjusted figure rather than being given the season mean and counted.
a per-season opponent baseline for most opponents opponent_adjusted.json 428 of the 649 opponent-seasons are a single match. The per-season table is exported for all of them, but its shrunk column at n = 1 is almost entirely the season mean, and the per-match season baseline is marked unavailable rather than silently pooled.
how much of a kill the setter, the passer or the digger is responsible for opponent_adjusted.json the box score prints one line per player per match and the play-by-play prints one terminal action per rally; no source records a touch inside a rally. rally_analytics refuses first-ball side-out for the same reason and nothing is imputed here either.
an opponent effect from an untrusted team block opponent_adjusted.json a block that does not reconcile with its own printed totals can have a player's numbers attached to the name above (handoff section 4). Those blocks contribute no value on their side and the row says which side is missing and why.
does a timeout stop a run? runs.json a timeout has no place in the rally sequence here. The Sidearm export keeps 571 timeout lines, but in a flat per-set 'other_lines' list with no score and no position among the rallies, and only for 56 of 146 pages; the huskers.com and StatCrew parsers keep none at all. Placing a timeout between two rallies is a parser change on all three sources, not something this record can be asked for.
what made a run happen - serving pressure, passing, blocking, the opponent falling apart? runs.json each rally prints exactly one line, the terminal action, and never a touch inside the rally. Attributing a run to a phase of play needs the touches, which no source here records - the same wall rally_analytics.json's first_ball_side_out runs into.
is a hitter hot - does a player who just got a kill get the next one? runs.json the record names the player who ended a rally but never the players who attempted and did not end one, so a per-player conversion rate has a numerator and no denominator. Serving is the one skill whose every attempt is on the record (a serve starts a rally), which is why the server is a stratum here and the hitter is not.
set length — refused era_adjusted.json rallies per set cannot be measured for 1975-1999: no season report for those years is parsed, and no play-by-play on disk starts before 2016. The side-out figure here is 2000's alone.
cannot be compared — refusal era_adjusted.json no player who played for Nebraska before 2000 appears anywhere in this file, in any table, at any rank. Not because she was worse - because no export on this machine holds a line for her. Every all-time table here is an all-time table of 2000-2025, and saying otherwise would be the error.

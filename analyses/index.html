Written 2026-09-17, after the independent statistics review of the five studies and the commits that applied its thirteen findings; section 5 and this header revised the same night, when the 2000-2015 box scores joined the corpus. This page is for a reader who is not an analyst. Every number on it comes from a file in data/reports/ (named in each section), every rate carries its own denominator and interval there, and the caveats are written here beside the claims rather than in a footnote somewhere else.

One match later (note added 2026-11-22). The exports now include the Creighton match of 2026-09-16 (857 box scores; 596 matches, 1,832 sets, 82,847 rallies, 45,608 serves in the rally studies). The prose below was written on 595 matches and is left as written; no verdict changed. What one match moved, from the exports: the serving exchange rate between players 0.341 -> 0.337 and within a player 0.162 -> 0.157, the break-even 0.948 -> 0.949, the detectable momentum bound 1.35 -> 1.34 points, and in the opponent-adjusted study the largest frozen-placebo gain for digs +0.004 -> +0.005 (a ninth of the real gain, where it was a tenth).

Twenty-two matches later (note added 2026-11-25). vb-latest brought in every match from 2026-09-18 (North Carolina) to 2026-11-24 (Purdue): 879 box scores; the rally studies read 616 matches, 1,898 sets, 85,615 rallies, 47,212 serves. Twenty of the 22 have a rally log that passes its checks; the huskers.com logs of 2026-10-15 Northwestern and 2026-10-30 Michigan do not, and Michigan's own Sidearm page - which passes all six - is set aside as a duplicate of that failing file (left open). Eight matches with both logs agree rally for rally; Minnesota differs on one rally whose PDF line lost its text. No verdict changed. What moved, from the exports:

  • Momentum (runs.json): the export's headline corrected estimate +0.43 -> +0.48 points [-0.59, +1.54], p 0.44 -> 0.38; one rally longer +0.80 -> +0.98, p 0.28 -> 0.18; the effect the corpus could detect with 80% power 1.34 -> 1.32 points; still 3 of 14 specifications significant, all of them the score-band one the file reports as a bound. Runs of three or more 5,184 -> 5,380, against 6,230 -> 6,462 when each set's rallies are shuffled.
  • Serving (serving.json): break-even 0.949 -> 0.953 aces per point of error; between players 0.337 -> 0.336 (bootstrap 0.165 to 0.299); within a player 0.157 -> 0.156 [0.054, 0.268] on the same 97 season pairs (2026 completes none until it ends); the serving package -598 -> -626 points.
  • Win probability (leverage.json): held-out Brier 0.1131 -> 0.1120 against margin-only 0.1186 -> 0.1175; calibration intervals still contain the prediction in 10 of 10 buckets (one bucket, 0.2-0.3, needs the clustering to, where two did). One label moved: the 2026 leader is no longer "not established". On ten matches she kept the lead in 43% of resamples, 0.16 sets of win probability ahead; on thirty, 74%, 1.44 sets ahead - "moderately firm".
  • Opponent-adjusted: 878 matches; the same 2 of 4 metrics survive, and the hitting-percentage gain still holds out of season (+0.00494 -> +0.00463, interval above zero). The largest frozen-placebo gain for digs fell from +0.005 to +0.002, a twenty-sixth of the real +0.054 where it was a ninth. Era-adjusted: unchanged (the official season reports end in 2025).
  • The 2026 rotations: with 10 matches the best and worst differed by 27 points of side-out (R6 87%, R1 60%); with 30, by 7 (R6 73.6% of 197, R1 66.5% of 340, intervals overlapping). The early gap was mostly small-sample noise.
  • A correction to section 1: "no source places a timeout among the rallies" is true only of the Sidearm pages. The StatCrew and huskers.com logs print timeouts between rallies (3,815 placed; bench_stats.json). A timeout does not change the next rally for either side: after Nebraska's 1,242 it won 794 against 804.2 expected (p = 0.54); after opponents' 2,297, p = 0.86.
  • One new correction (corr-0095): the huskers.com lineup reading gave Indiana's libero, #5 Audrey Jackson, to Nebraska's #15 Andi Jackson by surname on 2026-11-22; the claim is refused and recorded. The surname match itself is not fixed.

What the record is. Nebraska volleyball, 2000 to the first weeks of 2026: 856 box scores (the 530 StatCrew pages of 2000-2015 came in on Sept. 17; before that the record began in 2016), of which 595 matches (1,829 sets, 82,725 rallies, 2001 to 2026) also have a rally-by-rally play-by-play that passed every check: 148 matches of 2016-2026 and, since Sept. 18, 447 of the 483 StatCrew pages of 2001-2015 (statcrew_pbp.json: 1,663 sets, 1,389 pass the six checks; the 2000 season is side-out scoring, where a rally need not score, and is kept out of the rally studies on purpose). For the era study, the official season reports from 2000 to 2025: 341 player-season lines for 129 people. Nothing before 2000 has a per-player line in any file. Studies 1, 2 and 4 read only the rally record and study 3 rests on the official season reports, so the box scores did not move them (study 3 now records the wider player_seasons.json among its sources, nothing more); study 5 reads the box scores, and its verdicts did move. Sections 1, 2 and 4 were then re-read on the 26-season rally record; each says what it found on 2016-2026 alone where the answer changed. Two did: the serve-conversion verdict in section 2 and the strength of the momentum null in section 1.

What the record cannot show, in every study below. A rally is one printed line, the action that ended it: a kill, an error, an ace, a block. No dig, pass, set or block touch that kept a rally going is recorded anywhere, so none of them is measured anywhere. Every study says so in its own file; it is repeated here because it is the single biggest limit on all five.

Every number above is read out of a file in this repository, and every file is re-derived and re-checked by huskerstats vb-validate, which wrote data/reports/coverage.json at commit 5a554e5. Hover a file to read its checks.

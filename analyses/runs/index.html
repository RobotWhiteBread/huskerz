Scoring runs are not real

The question, as the export states it: does winning three rallies in a row raise the chance of winning the fourth?

no, not measurably: every stratification leaves an effect that is smaller than what this corpus could detect, and the naive version of the statistic is an artefact of the serving rule plus the arithmetic of conditioning on a streak
the export's own answer (answer)

The text below is this study's section of data/reports/findings.md, as written, for a reader who is not an analyst. The panel beside it is the export as it stands now; where the two differ, the studies page says what has changed since the text was written.

What it measures. Whether winning the last few rallies makes Nebraska more likely to win the next one - "momentum", a hot streak, a run that feeds itself.

What it found: still no, with a smaller bound and a warmer estimate. Over 82,725 rallies of 1,829 sets, having won the last three rallies Nebraska wins the fourth 50.5% of the time (4,608 such rallies); on a serve that starts a fresh turn it wins 46.4% (24,736 rallies). That 4.1-point gap looks like momentum. Most of it is not, for two reasons the study measures:

  • The serving rule. In rally scoring the team that wins a rally serves the next one - true on 80,896 of 80,896 consecutive rallies in the record, no exception. So "Nebraska won the last rally" and "Nebraska is serving" are the same event, and serving is the weaker position: Nebraska wins 47.4% of the rallies it serves against a base rate of 55.6%, so having won the last rally lowers the chance of winning the next by 8.3 points.
  • Arithmetic. Shuffling the order of each set's own rallies at random - keeping every set's final score exactly as it was - produces 3.2 points of that 4.1-point gap on its own. What is left is +0.87 points, with a 95% interval from -0.6 to +2.3 points and p = 0.24.

Fourteen ways of asking the question were tried. On 2016-2026 none crossed 5% and the estimates scattered around zero (+0.65 at three won, -0.3 at four). On 26 seasons every one of the fourteen is positive, between +0.1 and +1.9 points; the tightest fair specification (inside one match and one server) reads +1.4 points at p = 0.07, and the three that do cross 5% all hold the score band fixed, which the streak itself moves, so the file reports them as a bound rather than adopting them. The honest reading: if there is momentum in this record it is worth about a point of serve-win rate, and the record cannot yet tell that from zero.

The strongest statement still runs the other way: Nebraska's 5,173 runs of three or more points are fewer than the 6,220 a random reshuffle of the same sets produces, because winning a rally hands over the serve.

How sure. This is a null with a measured bound, not a proof of zero. An effect smaller than about 1.35 points of serve-win rate could hide in these 45,535 serves and the test would miss it four times in five (2.75 points on 2016-2026: four times the serves, half the bound). The study injected fake streak effects into the real serving structure to check it would find them: at 2.5 points it did 98% of the time, at 5 points every time.

What it cannot see. Whether a timeout stops a run (no source places a timeout among the rallies). What made a run happen. Whether a hitter is hot: the record names who ended a rally but never who attempted and did not.

What the review changed. The "could hide" bound above was 2.47 points; it is 2.75 now. The old figure came from the permutation test's own spread, which conditions on each set's final score and is therefore a little too tight for this one purpose. The bound is now taken from a resample of whole matches (4,000 draws), which is 11% wider. The estimate and its p-value did not move.

What the export says · verdict

  • Naively, Nebraska wins 0.4942 of the rallies that follow three it has just won (9873 rallies), against a base rate of 0.5575 over all 85615. That is -0.0633, and it is negative because winning hands over the serve.
  • Inside the serving state the gap runs the other way: having won the last three rallies (two serves already won) Nebraska takes 0.4942 of the fourth, on 10112 of them, against 0.4656 on a serve that starts a turn (25597): +0.0285.
  • But a within-set reshuffle of the same serves produces +0.0238 of that on its own. What is left - the answer to the question - is +0.0048 [-0.0059, +0.0154], p = 0.380.
  • One rally longer - four already won, so the fifth - the corrected estimate is +0.0098 [-0.0044, +0.0240], p = 0.176, on 4807 rallies.
  • Nebraska's 5380 runs of three or more are fewer than the 6462 the same sets produce when the order of their own rallies is shuffled at random. Long runs are not evidence of momentum until the serving rule is taken out, because the team that wins a rally has to serve the next one, and serving is worth less than receiving.
  • the three streak indicators change held-out log loss by -0.000000 nats per rally, and help in 14 of 26 held-out seasons. A term that mattered would help in most of them.
  • 14 specifications, 3 significant at 5%. The closest is 'match_server_score' asking about rally 3 (having won the last 2), corrected +0.0131, p = 0.000 - and it is the one that shuffles inside bands of the score, which the streak itself moves, so it is reported here rather than adopted. Every corrected estimate in the table is smaller than the 0.0132 this corpus could have detected with 80% power.

Questions it refuses

  • does a timeout stop a run? — a timeout has no place in the rally sequence here. The Sidearm export keeps 571 timeout lines, but in a flat per-set 'other_lines' list with no score and no position among the rallies, and only for 56 of 145 pages; the huskers.com and StatCrew parsers keep none at all. Placing a timeout between two rallies is a parser change on all three sources, not something this record can be asked for.
  • what made a run happen - serving pressure, passing, blocking, the opponent falling apart? — each rally prints exactly one line, the terminal action, and never a touch inside the rally. Attributing a run to a phase of play needs the touches, which no source here records - the same wall rally_analytics.json's first_ball_side_out runs into.
  • is a hitter hot - does a player who just got a kill get the next one? — the record names the player who ended a rally but never the players who attempted and did not end one, so a per-player conversion rate has a numerator and no denominator. Serving is the one skill whose every attempt is on the record (a serve starts a rally), which is why the server is a stratum here and the hitter is not.

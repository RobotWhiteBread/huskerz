Opponent-adjusted performance, for the two numbers it works on

The question, as the export states it: what each opponent does and allows, and Nebraska's matches, seasons and players expressed against it

The text below is this study's section of data/reports/findings.md, as written, for a reader who is not an analyst. The panel beside it is the export as it stands now; where the two differ, the studies page says what has changed since the text was written.

What it measures. Nebraska's hitting percentage, kills, digs and blocks per set in each match, against what that opponent allowed in Nebraska's other meetings with them, so a .350 against Wisconsin and a .350 against Rutgers read differently.

What it found, and the premise it corrects first. Every one of the 856 box scores is a Nebraska match, so "what an opponent allows" means "what Nebraska did to them the other times" - this is a fixture history, not a scouting report, and a true strength of schedule is refused outright. Within that, on 27 seasons: knowing the opponent predicts Nebraska's hitting percentage and her digs per set, and nothing else. Leave a match out and fit on the other seasons, the adjustment cuts the error in hitting percentage from 0.0801 to 0.0751 (gain 0.0050, 95% interval 0.0024 to 0.0075), and 0.0049 (0.0023 to 0.0074) when nothing at all from the held-out season is allowed in; digs per set from 2.90 to 2.85 (gain 0.050, 0.011 to 0.087; 0.045 with the season held out). Kills and blocks gain nothing distinguishable from zero. The adjustment barely reorders the tops of the tables: Andi Jackson's 2025 is first in hitting percentage either way and Dani Busboom's 2006 first in digs either way.

This is a different verdict from the one the same study gave on 2016-2026 alone, and the reason is the corpus, not the method. On eleven seasons hitting percentage gained more (0.0081) and digs gained and then lost their interval under the home/away control; the 2000-2011 Big 12 seasons meet every conference opponent twice a season, so the per-opponent baselines rest on 796 matches instead of 282, and the dig interval clears zero on every design (0.044, 0.003 to 0.085, within home and away). The per-season basis, which was the weaker one before, now fails for every metric.

The opposite question works much better: knowing the opponent predicts what they do (their kills per set: error 2.53 down to 2.30), because Nebraska is the constant in every match and the opponent is what varies.

How sure. The two metrics that work clear four checks: the held-out test, a version where nothing from the held-out season enters, a placebo with shuffled opponent labels pushed through the same weighting, and the home/away control. The rankings for the two metrics that fail are still published, because the arithmetic is real, and each carries the word NOISE on its own row and in its CSV column name.

What it cannot see. 55 of the 136 opponents were met exactly once, so those 55 matches, plus the four whose Nebraska block does not add up, carry no baseline. Nothing about how strong these opponents were against anyone else.

What the review changed. The block called "held out by season" was not one: the opponent's other matches from the held-out season were still in the baseline. It is renamed, a genuine season holdout sits beside it (the 0.0077 above), and the verdict requires both. The placebo was close to circular: shuffled labels drove the weighting to zero, so a gain of zero was arithmetic; a second placebo that keeps the real weighting can and does produce spurious gains of the size of the real kills gain, and hitting percentage still clears it. And the three failing metrics used to be exported in exactly the shape of the one that works, with the warning only in the code; the warning is now on every table and in every column header.

What the export says · read_this_first

  • all 879 matches used from box_scores.json have a Nebraska block, so every observation of an opponent is an observation against Nebraska. 'What they allow' is a fixture history, not a scouting report.
  • leave one out: the baseline for a match never includes that match
  • 'season' is the opponent's other matches that season (445 of the 875 matches with a trusted Nebraska line have one); 'career' is their other matches in any season with each season's mean removed first (818 have one; the other 57 are opponents this corpus holds exactly once)
  • hitting percentage in percentage points (value - beta); kills, digs and blocks per set multiplicatively by grand/(grand + beta), which assumes the opponent effect scales every player in proportion to her own production - an assumption, not a measurement
  • rounding: nothing in this file is rounded
  • 'allows' is Nebraska's own line in that match, filed under the opponent who gave it up, so above_baseline > 0 means Nebraska beat that fixture's history. 'does' is the opponent's line, so above_baseline > 0 means the opponent beat it and Nebraska's defence was below its own history; 'does.adjusted' is what an average offence would have produced against Nebraska that night.

Questions it refuses

  • strength of schedule against the rest of the country — every match in data/reports/box_scores.json is a Nebraska match, so no opponent's games against a third team are anywhere in this corpus. What is computed here is relative to the opponents Nebraska itself played, and nothing in it can say whether those opponents are strong nationally.
  • an opponent effect for a team we hold once — 57 of the 138 opponents appear in exactly one match. Leaving that match out leaves nothing, so those matches carry no baseline and are excluded from every adjusted figure rather than being given the season mean and counted.
  • a per-season opponent baseline for most opponents — 428 of the 649 opponent-seasons are a single match. The per-season table is exported for all of them, but its shrunk column at n = 1 is almost entirely the season mean, and the per-match season baseline is marked unavailable rather than silently pooled.
  • how much of a kill the setter, the passer or the digger is responsible for — the box score prints one line per player per match and the play-by-play prints one terminal action per rally; no source records a touch inside a rally. rally_analytics refuses first-ball side-out for the same reason and nothing is imputed here either.
  • an opponent effect from an untrusted team block — a block that does not reconcile with its own printed totals can have a player's numbers attached to the name above (handoff section 4). Those blocks contribute no value on their side and the row says which side is missing and why.
